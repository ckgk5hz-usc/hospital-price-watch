Hospital prices · Story

One hospital stay, two prices

What commercial insurers paid California hospitals for a stay, against Medicare, against each other, and against quality ratings.

Skip to the charts

1 of 5

Start with Medicare

For a hospital stay, adjusted for how sick the patient was, Medicare paid the median California hospital $10,214 in 2023.

HCAI Hospital Measurement dataset, 2023 reporting year. Median across hospitals.

2 of 5

Commercial insurers paid about twice as much

For the same kind of stay, commercial insurers paid the median hospital $21,355: 2.05 times Medicare.

Ratio is the median of each hospital's own ratio.

3 of 5

But hospitals are far apart

Each dot is one of 236 general-acute hospitals with at least 100 commercial stays. A hospital at the 90th percentile was paid $34,483 per stay, 3.5 times one at the 10th percentile ($9,740).

Payments above $60,000 are drawn at the right edge. Part of the spread reflects patient mix the adjustment misses.

4 of 5

Paying more is not the same as rating higher

Sorted by their federal quality rating, higher-rated hospitals were paid more on average: a median of $26,766 at 5 stars against $13,425 at 1 star. That is a pattern, not proof that price buys quality: larger teaching hospitals tend to rate higher and treat harder cases.

CMS Overall Hospital Quality Star Rating, 2026-07-22. Dashed ticks mark each group's median.

5 of 5

Against each hospital's own Medicare rate

Compared with what Medicare paid the same hospital, commercial insurers paid the median hospital 2.05 times as much, and 22 hospitals more than 3 times as much (orange).

Ratios above 6x are drawn at the right edge.
