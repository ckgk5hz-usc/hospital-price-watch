Hospital Price Watch brief

Hospital prices

What do insurers pay hospitals, and why does it vary so much?

Data as of 2026-07-22. Companion to the USC Center for Health Financing, Policy, and Management. Every finding links to its chart and source.

Key findings

  • Commercial insurers paid the median California hospital $21,355 per adjusted inpatient stay, 2.05 times what Medicare paid.1
  • A hospital at the 90th percentile was paid $34,483 per commercial stay, 3.5 times one at the 10th percentile.2
  • Private plans paid hospitals 253% of Medicare prices in 2022 (state-level median).3

What hospitals are paid

What commercial insurers and Medicare paid California hospitals, and how far apart hospitals are.

  • Commercial insurers paid the median California hospital $21,355 per adjusted inpatient stay, 2.05 times what Medicare paid.1
  • For outpatient visits, commercial insurers paid the median hospital 2.31 times Medicare.4
  • A hospital at the 90th percentile was paid $34,483 per commercial stay, 3.5 times one at the 10th percentile.2

Price and quality

Whether hospitals paid more rate higher on federal quality measures.

  • Hospitals with higher federal quality ratings were paid more: $26,766 per commercial stay at 5 stars, against $13,425 at 1 star (medians).5

National benchmarks

How hospital prices compare across the country and over time.

  • Private plans paid hospitals 253% of Medicare prices in 2022 (state-level median).3
  • Since April 2019, hospital prices paid by private insurance rose 30%, against 21% for Medicare.6

Market power

Research on why some hospitals can charge more.

  • After Sutter Health adopted "all-or-nothing" contracting, its hospital prices stayed 30% above those at comparable control hospitals.7
  • Trauma-center hospitals charged 4.4% more for non-trauma inpatient stays, and 5.2% more for non-trauma emergency visits.8
