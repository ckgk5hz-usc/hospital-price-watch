Hospital finances · Story

Who is losing money

California's general-acute hospitals in fiscal year 2023: how many lost money on operations, and which kinds of hospitals did worst.

Skip to the chartsSimple report

1 of 5

272 hospitals, one year

Each dot is one of California's 272 general-acute hospitals that filed a full-year financial report with the state for fiscal year 2023.

HCAI annual financial disclosures, FY2023.

2 of 5

More than half lost money

151 of them (56%) spent more running the hospital than they took in from patient care and other operations. Investment income can still leave some of them in the black overall.

Operating margin below zero. Same group as the explorer's default view.

3 of 5

Most cluster near break-even

Lined up by operating margin, most hospitals sit within a few points of zero. The median was -1.5%; a few lost or made far more.

Margins beyond -40% or +30% are drawn at the edge.

4 of 5

Ownership makes a difference

Grouped by who owns them, the median investor-owned hospital ran +0.6%, and the median city or county hospital -11.3%. Small groups move with one or two hospitals.

Ownership as HCAI classifies it in its latest year.

5 of 5

Safety-net hospitals are under more pressure

Hospitals that qualify for Medicare payments for serving many low-income patients had a median margin of -3.1%, against -0.1% at other general-acute hospitals.

Safety-net: Medicare disproportionate share (DSH) status in the HCAI panel.
