Glossary

What the terms on this site mean, in plain words, and where to see each one used.

Hospital financesHospitalsPricesCoverage and premiumsSpending and affordabilityData and sources

Hospital finances

Operating margin
Operating income (operating revenue minus operating expenses) as a share of operating revenue. It leaves out income from investments, donations and other nonoperating sources. Where it appears
Total margin
Net income, including investment income, donations and other nonoperating revenue, as a share of total revenue. Where it appears
Gross charges
A hospital's list prices for everything it provided, before any discount. Few payers pay them; they are the starting point for negotiated and government rates. Where it appears
Net patient revenue
What a hospital expects to collect for patient care after discounts to insurers and government programs, charity care and bad debt are taken off its gross charges. Where it appears
Charges per dollar of net revenue
Gross charges divided by net patient revenue. A value of 4.0 means the hospital listed $4 in charges for every $1 it expects to collect. Where it appears
Payer mix
The share of a hospital's gross charges billed to each payer: Medicare, Medi-Cal, private insurance, county programs and others. It is based on charges, not on what each payer actually pays. Where it appears
Bad debt
Bills a hospital expected patients to pay that went unpaid. Where it appears
Charity care
Care a hospital provided free or at a discount under its financial assistance policy, to patients it judged unable to pay. Where it appears
Uncompensated care
On this site, bad debt plus charity care, as a share of gross charges. Where it appears
Outpatient share
The share of a hospital's gross patient revenue that comes from outpatient care (visits, procedures and tests without an overnight admission). Where it appears
Fiscal year (FY)
On this site, the year a hospital's reporting period ends. A hospital whose year runs July 2023 to June 2024 reports FY2024. Where it appears

Hospitals

General acute care hospital
A hospital that provides round-the-clock inpatient medical and surgical care. The comparisons on this site leave out psychiatric, rehabilitation and long-term acute care hospitals. Where it appears
Safety-net hospital (DSH)
On this site, a hospital that qualifies for Medicare disproportionate share hospital (DSH) payments, which go to hospitals treating a large share of low-income patients. Where it appears
Critical access hospital
A small rural hospital with no more than 25 inpatient beds, usually far from another hospital, that Medicare pays based on its costs. Where it appears
Health system
A group of hospitals, and often physician groups and clinics, under common ownership or management. Where it appears
CMS star rating
The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services' Overall Hospital Quality Star Rating: one to five stars summarizing measures of mortality, safety, readmissions, patient experience and timely care. Hospitals that report too few measures get no rating. Where it appears

Prices

Commercial insurance
Coverage from private insurers, through an employer or bought individually, including Covered California plans. Where it appears
Case-mix adjusted stay
An inpatient stay weighted by how resource-intensive the patient's condition is, so a hospital treating sicker patients can be compared with one treating healthier ones. Where it appears
Price compared with Medicare
What commercial insurers paid divided by what Medicare paid for comparable care at the same hospital. 2.0 means commercial insurers paid twice Medicare's rate. Where it appears
Medicare
The federal health insurance program for people 65 and older and some younger people with disabilities. Traditional Medicare sets hospital payment rates rather than negotiating them; Medicare Advantage plans are run by private insurers. Where it appears
Medi-Cal
California's Medicaid program, which covers people with low incomes. Most members are enrolled in managed care plans. Where it appears

Coverage and premiums

Premium
The amount paid for health coverage, usually monthly. For employer plans, the total premium is split between the employer and the worker. Where it appears
Worker contribution
The part of an employer plan's premium that the employee pays, usually through payroll deduction. Where it appears
Deductible
What a person pays for covered care each year before the plan starts paying, apart from services the plan covers in full. Where it appears
High-deductible health plan
A plan whose deductible is at least the minimum the IRS sets for pairing it with a health savings account. Where it appears
Covered California
California's health insurance marketplace under the Affordable Care Act, where people without job-based coverage buy plans, often with federal and state help. Where it appears
Benchmark Silver plan
The second-lowest-cost Silver plan available in an area. Federal premium tax credits are calculated from its price. Where it appears
Premium tax credit
A federal subsidy that lowers Marketplace premiums for eligible people based on income. California adds its own help for some enrollees. Where it appears

Spending and affordability

Office of Health Care Affordability (OHCA)
An office within HCAI, created by state law in 2022, that measures health spending in California and sets and enforces limits on how fast it may grow. Where it appears
Spending growth target
OHCA's limit on yearly growth in health spending per person, starting at 3.5% for 2025 and stepping down to 3.0% by 2029. Where it appears
Per member per year (PMPY)
Spending divided by the number of people covered, for a year, so markets and regions of different sizes can be compared. Where it appears
Rating region
One of the 19 geographic areas Covered California uses to set premiums. OHCA reports spending using the same regions. Where it appears
True cost of economic security
The Urban Institute's measure of what a family needs to cover essentials without hardship and to save for emergencies and the future. It is set higher than the federal poverty line. Where it appears
Delinquency
A debt payment that is past due. The district page reports the share of people whose credit card or auto loan payments are 60 or more days late. Where it appears

Data and sources

HCAI
The California Department of Health Care Access and Information, which collects hospitals' annual financial disclosures and publishes the Hospital Measurement dataset used for prices on this site. Where it appears
MEPS-IC
The Insurance Component of the federal Medical Expenditure Panel Survey: a yearly survey of private employers on premiums, contributions and deductibles, with estimates for every state. Where it appears
Median
The middle value when hospitals or places are ranked. Half are above and half below, so a few extreme values do not move it. Where it appears
Preliminary
Data the source may still revise. On this site FY2024 hospital figures are preliminary. Where it appears
Congressional district
One of California's 52 areas that each elect a member of the U.S. House. This site uses the boundaries in effect for the 119th Congress. Where it appears

Definitions describe how this site uses each term. For the full methods behind a figure, see the source named under each chart and Sources and how we check them.
