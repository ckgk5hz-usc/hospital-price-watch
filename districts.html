How to read this

  • Premium. Unsubsidized monthly premium of the second-lowest-cost ("benchmark") Silver plan on the ACA Marketplace for a 40-year-old nonsmoker, averaged across the district by population (Urban Institute technical appendix). It is the sticker price before subsidies. Most Covered California members get federal or state help and pay much less. Dollars are not adjusted for inflation.
  • Why it differs from KFF. Urban averages premiums by population; KFF averages by plan enrollment, so KFF's California benchmark for 2026 is a little higher. Covered California sets prices for 19 rating regions; Urban gives each county its region's price and averages the counties in a district by population.
  • Cost of economic security. The share of people in families whose resources fall below what it takes to pay for the goods and services needed to take full part in today's economy and society, and to save for emergencies and the future (Urban Institute, 2023 data). Urban advises caution when comparing it across years.
  • Credit card delinquency. Share of people with credit/charge card debt who are 60 or more days delinquent. From Urban's credit bureau panel, a sample of credit records, not a survey.
  • Districts. Boundaries of the 119th Congress are used for every year, so earlier years describe today's district area. ZIP matches use land area from the Census Bureau, so a ZIP split between districts shows each share. The page names districts, not their representatives.
  • Districts often share a value. Urban builds each district's premium from county prices, and Covered California sets one price for a whole region, so districts inside the same county usually get the same number. For 2026, 10 districts (all within Los Angeles County) show $461 a month. Read a district's premium as its county's or region's price, and the ranking as a rough guide.
  • Hospitals are not shown by district. This site places hospitals at city centers, which is not precise enough to assign them to districts.
