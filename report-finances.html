Hospital Price Watch brief

Hospital finances

Are California hospitals making money, and which ones are struggling?

Data as of 2026-10-11. Companion to the USC Center for Health Financing, Policy, and Management. Every finding links to its chart and source.

Key findings

  • 56% of California general-acute hospitals lost money on operations in FY2023.1
  • Median operating margin was −1.5% in FY2023 and +0.6% in preliminary FY2024.2
  • Safety-net hospitals had a median FY2023 operating margin of −3.1%, against −0.1% at other general-acute hospitals.3

Margins

How many hospitals lose money on patient care, and the median result year by year.

  • 56% of California general-acute hospitals lost money on operations in FY2023.1
  • Median operating margin was −1.5% in FY2023 and +0.6% in preliminary FY2024.2

Who is struggling

How results differ by ownership and for safety-net hospitals.

  • In FY2023 the median investor-owned hospital ran a +0.6% operating margin; the median city or county hospital, −11.3%.4
  • Safety-net hospitals had a median FY2023 operating margin of −3.1%, against −0.1% at other general-acute hospitals.3

Where revenue comes from

Which payers hospitals depend on, and the shift to outpatient care.

  • Medicare was the largest payer at 178 of 276 general-acute hospitals in FY2023, and Medi-Cal at 85.5
  • Outpatient care made up 46% of gross patient revenue at the median hospital in FY2024, up from 37% in FY2014.6

Free care

Bad debt and charity care as a share of charges.

  • The median general-acute hospital wrote off 1.7% of billed charges as bad debt or charity care in FY2023.7
