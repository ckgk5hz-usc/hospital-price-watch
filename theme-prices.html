Price and quality

Whether hospitals paid more rate higher on federal quality measures.

Computed from dataUpdated 2026-07-22

Hospitals with higher federal quality ratings were paid more: $26,766 per commercial stay at 5 stars, against $13,425 at 1 star (medians).

CMS Overall Hospital Quality Star Rating (2026-07-22 release), joined by CMS number through the state facility listing. Hospitals per group: 24, 50, 59, 64, 24. A pattern, not a cause: larger teaching hospitals tend to rate higher and treat more complex cases. HCAI Hospital Measurement dataset, 2023 reporting year, computed on this site.See the full table

Market power

Research on why some hospitals can charge more.

1 of 2
Published findingUpdated 2024-07-01

After Sutter Health adopted "all-or-nothing" contracting, its hospital prices stayed 30% above those at comparable control hospitals.

Melnick & Fonkych, Journal of Hospital Management and Health Policy, 2024 (USC Schaeffer Center summary, July 1, 2024). The 30% is the gap to control hospitals, not Sutter's own before-and-after change.Read the source

Published findingUpdated 2024-03-05

Trauma-center hospitals charged 4.4% more for non-trauma inpatient stays, and 5.2% more for non-trauma emergency visits.

Kessler, Sweeney & Melnick, Health Affairs 43(3), March 2024: commercial claims, 2012 to 2018, about 2,000 hospitals nationwide. The 4.4% is for inpatient admissions; 5.2% is for emergency department visits in the authors’ baseline model (2.7% in a sensitivity model).Read the paper
