The site

Hospital Price Watch collects California hospital financial data and the research around hospital prices and health care affordability in one place. It is built on the Center's published work and on the state's public hospital disclosures. It is a prototype and is not yet on its own domain.

The Center

The Center for Health Financing, Policy, and Management was established in 1996 within the USC Sol Price School of Public Policy. It is led by Glenn Melnick, PhD, Blue Cross of California Chair in Health Care Finance and Senior Scholar at the USC Leonard D. Schaeffer Center for Health Policy & Economics. Its mission is to expand knowledge that improves health care, health system performance and the management of health services.

How we source numbers

Primary data

Every hospital-level statistic comes directly from California's Hospital Annual Financial Disclosure Data, collected by the Department of Health Care Access and Information (HCAI). Hospitals report income statements, payer mix and utilization every year.

Published research

Every other finding is cited by name: journal or outlet, author, year. We do not restate a finding without saying where it came from, and exhibits that restate a finding link to it.

When we build on someone else's method

If this site applies an analytical framework developed elsewhere, such as a financial-distress index or an affordability model, we say so directly: method adapted from [source], applied to California data by Hospital Price Watch. A borrowed method, openly credited, is still real research. The same method with the source removed is not.

News

News and commentary are picked by hand and checked before they are listed. The site has no backend, so the list does not update itself.

Data and limits

  • 449 California hospitals, fiscal years 2022 to 2024, plus an operating-margin panel back to 2020. A fiscal year is the year the reporting period ends.
  • FY2024 figures are preliminary. Reports covering less than a full year are left out of yearly figures.
  • Map positions are city centers from the Census Gazetteer, not street addresses. 27 hospitals could not be placed and are not on the map.
  • Medians are used throughout so a few very large or very small hospitals do not drive results.
