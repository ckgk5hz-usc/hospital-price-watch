Hospital Price Watch brief

Affordability

What do households pay for health coverage, and who is falling behind?

Data as of 2026-11-27. Companion to the USC Center for Health Financing, Policy, and Management. Every finding links to its chart and source.

Key findings

  • 59% of Californians said they or a family member skipped or delayed care because of cost in the past year.1
  • The benchmark Marketplace premium for a 40-year-old rose 12% in California in 2026, to $548 a month, against 22% nationally.2
  • After inflation, California's average employer family premium rose 16% from 2016 to 2025; median household income rose 19%.3

Employer coverage

Most Californians under 65 get coverage through a job. What it costs, who pays, and how fast it has grown.

  • After inflation, California's average employer family premium rose 16% from 2016 to 2025; median household income rose 19%.3
  • California's average employer family premium, $27,057 in 2025, equaled 27% of median household income.4
  • California workers paid $7,847 toward family coverage in 2025, up from $4,829 in 2016.5
  • The average single deductible in California employer plans reached $1,801 in 2025, against $1,476 in 2016.6
  • 40% of single-coverage enrollees in California private-sector employer plans were in a high-deductible plan in 2025, against 54% nationally.7
  • California employer family coverage averaged $28,397 in 2025, against $26,993 nationally.8
  • Employer family coverage cost $28,303 in 2026, up 4.9% in a year. Workers paid $7,471.9
  • Large-group premium rates in DMHC-regulated plans rose 10.7% in 2024, the most in the decade shown, and 6.5% in 2025.10

Marketplace plans

What a plan bought on Covered California costs before subsidies, and how that differs by district.

  • The benchmark Marketplace premium for a 40-year-old rose 12% in California in 2026, to $548 a month, against 22% nationally.2
  • California's benchmark Marketplace premium for a 40-year-old went from $321 a month in 2017 to $548 in 2026.11
  • In 2026 the benchmark premium ran from $791 a month in Chico & Redding (CA-1) to $461 in 10 districts (all within Los Angeles County).12

Households under strain

What people do when costs are too high: skip care, carry medical bills, fall short of a secure budget.

  • 59% of Californians said they or a family member skipped or delayed care because of cost in the past year.1
  • 6.6% of Californians had trouble paying medical bills in 2023-2024, against 11.3% nationally.13
  • 11.6% of California adults could not get medical care when needed because of cost in 2024 (U.S.: 12.4%).14
  • 54% of Californians lived in families below the true cost of economic security in 2023, against 49% nationally.15
