Access to care · Story

Where care is disappearing

Birth units that closed, hospitals that shut, and Californians who go without care because of what it costs.

Skip to the chartsSimple report

1 of 7

240 hospitals that delivered babies

Each dot is one of 240 California hospitals that reported at least 100 natural (non-cesarean) births in at least one year from 2012 to 2023.

HCAI annual financial disclosures. Natural births leave out cesarean deliveries, so they undercount all births.

2 of 7

21 stopped for good

21 of them later reported no natural births and kept reporting none. That points to a closed birth unit, though the unit may have closed earlier in the year before.

Inferred from state filings, not from announcements. CalMatters counted 56 hospitals that stopped delivering babies since 2012, using a broader method.

3 of 7

Most since 2020

17 of the 21 stopped in 2020 or later. The 6 lighter dots in 2024 rest on preliminary 2024 data and could change.

First fiscal year with no natural births. FY2024 data are preliminary.

4 of 7

Fewer hospitals delivering, and fewer births

The number of hospitals reporting any natural births peaked at 235 in 2015 and was 213 in 2023. Births fell too: natural births dropped 11% from 2012 to 2023.

Hospitals with one or more natural births in the year, from HCAI disclosures. The lighter 2024 bar is preliminary.

5 of 7

Closures are rarer than they look

59 California hospitals stopped filing state financial reports after 2012. Checked one by one in news, state and county records, 18 had closed or ended inpatient care. Most of the rest had a new owner, name or license.

This site's research on each hospital, listed with sources in the explorer's closures layer.

6 of 7

Los Angeles County lost the most

Of the 18 verified closures, 8 were in Los Angeles County. 3 of the 18 were state developmental centers rather than community hospitals.

Year the hospital closed or ended inpatient care.

7 of 7

And people go without

In 2024, 11.6% of California adults said they could not get medical care when they needed it because of the cost, against 12.4% nationally. That is about 12 in 100.

SHADAC State Health Compare, from the CDC Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System. California margin of error ±1.0 points.
